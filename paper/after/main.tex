\begin{abstract}
Dietary self-monitoring has been used in behavioral weight-management interventions, yet adherence definitions and usage--outcome relationships remain heterogeneous across studies. In Taiwan, everyday meals frequently involve mixed dishes, colloquial names, and underspecified portion descriptions that challenge conventional database-search workflows and invite false precision. This paper presents DietSense, a prospective research proposal for a Traditional Chinese conversational domain AI agent designed for traceable dietary logging and nutrition analysis in Taiwan. Rather than allowing a large language model (LLM) to generate nutrient values from parametric memory, DietSense enforces an explicit trust boundary that decouples natural-language interpretation and workflow orchestration from versioned data retrieval and deterministic computation. Its eight-module conceptual architecture treats user utterances and retrieved text as untrusted inputs, retrieves candidate records from USDA FoodData Central and versioned Taiwan food-composition and local-recipe sources, and delegates energy and macronutrient arithmetic to pure deterministic functions while strictly distinguishing \texttt{null}/\texttt{unknown} fields from zero. When food identity, preparation state, or portion mass is insufficiently specified, a non-blocking state machine executes bounded clarification or degrades gracefully to partial results, evidence-bounded plausible ranges (explicitly distinguished from calibrated statistical confidence intervals), or text-only logs. Versioned safety guardrails restrict outputs to general informational support and exclude clinical diagnosis or dietary prescription. We define a prospective evaluation protocol comprising 500 stratified Traditional Chinese meal descriptions compared against a fixed manual-search baseline and a 20-adult, 7-day feasibility study. No system implementation, data collection, or empirical results are reported at this proposal stage.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Global and local public-health statistics motivate research into practical dietary self-monitoring tools. The World Health Organization (WHO) estimates that approximately 2.6 billion adults worldwide were overweight in 2024, including 940 million adults living with obesity~\cite{who2026obesity}. In Taiwan, the Health Promotion Administration (HPA) reported that 51.3\% of adults aged 18 years or older met the combined overweight-or-obesity criterion in the 2020--2024 National Nutrition and Health Survey~\cite{hpa2026obesity}. These prevalence estimates establish public-health relevance without implying that calorie logging alone prevents or treats disease. A systematic review of 59 behavioral weight-loss interventions found that dietary self-monitoring may support weight management, but inconsistent adherence metrics limit conclusions about how monitoring intensity relates to outcomes~\cite{raber2021systematic}.

Conventional database-driven food logging requires users to search food entries, reconcile raw-versus-cooked states, and estimate portion weights. Systematic reviews demonstrate that portion-size estimation aids vary widely in validation quality~\cite{amoutzopoulos2020portion}, and image-based dietary assessment methods exhibit substantial inter-study heterogeneity~\cite{ho2020validity}. Although authoritative food composition databases exist---including USDA FoodData Central (FDC)~\cite{usda_fdc} and the Taiwan Food and Drug Administration (TFDA) Food Nutrition Composition Database~\cite{tfda_database}---everyday Taiwanese meal descriptions such as ``one bowl of beef noodle soup with egg, half the broth consumed'' omit bowl volume, noodle mass, meat cut, cooking oil, and broth intake. Returning a single precise calorie number from a superficially matched database entry conceals these portion and recipe assumptions.

Large language models (LLMs) and retrieval-augmented generation (RAG)~\cite{lewis2020rag} enable flexible language parsing and tool coordination~\cite{yao2023react}. Recent nutrition informatics research has already explored multimodal RAG over U.S. food databases~\cite{yan2025dietai24}, free-text food mapping~\cite{lemay2026foodmapper}, retrieval-augmented food classification~\cite{zhou2026nutrirag}, domain-aligned food LLMs~\cite{gjorgjevikj2026foodyllm}, text-based nutrient estimation against U.S. and Chinese composition tables~\cite{lawabni2026text,cao2026chinese}, and image-based dietary assessment in Taiwan~\cite{chen2025diabot}. Accordingly, we do not claim global priority in combining LLMs, RAG, or Chinese food estimation. Instead, we formulate a bounded, empirically testable research gap: whether an end-to-end Traditional Chinese conversational workflow integrating structured retrieval, bounded multi-turn clarification, deterministic calculation, and field-level provenance can produce auditable nutrition estimates for Taiwanese mixed dishes while quantifying estimation error, provenance compliance, and interaction burden against a fixed manual-search baseline.

This proposal makes four prospective contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item A trust-bounded eight-module conceptual architecture for Traditional Chinese dietary logging that isolates LLM parsing from deterministic numeric authority.
    \item A deterministic nutrient-scaling and evidence-bounded plausible-range formulation that distinguishes \texttt{unknown} from zero and prohibits uncalibrated confidence intervals.
    \item Paired non-blocking multi-intent request handling and bounded clarification-resumption algorithms with guaranteed termination and versioned safety gates.
    \item A preregistration-ready prospective evaluation design incorporating a 500-item stratified Taiwanese meal benchmark, four-tier reference labeling, cluster-aware statistical analysis, and a 20-adult, 7-day feasibility study.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\subsection{Food Language Understanding and Entity Linking}
Food natural language processing (NLP) distinguishes surface entity extraction from standardized database linking. Popovski et al.~\cite{popovski2019foodbase} introduced the FoodBase corpus of curated recipe annotations, and Stojanov et al.~\cite{stojanov2021foodner} fine-tuned BERT and BioBERT models for food named-entity recognition (NER), reporting macro-F1 scores of 73.39\%--78.96\% across ontology subsets. Feher et al.~\cite{feher2023fewshot} compared string matching, classical classifiers, and few-shot Siamese models for food entity linking (89.40\% hybrid accuracy on E.Care concepts). Ogrinc et al.~\cite{ogrinc2024zeroshot} evaluated ChatGPT-3.5 and GPT-4 in zero-shot food NER and ontology linking across recipe and abstract corpora. However, these studies focus on English recipes or abstracts rather than colloquial Traditional Chinese meal logs, and entity linking alone does not resolve portion mass or nutrient provenance.

\subsection{RAG, Tool-Using Agents, and LLM Nutrition Estimation}
Lewis et al.~\cite{lewis2020rag} formalized RAG for knowledge-intensive NLP, and Yao et al.~\cite{yao2023react} introduced ReAct to interleave reasoning with external tool actions; neither validates nutrition accuracy or clinical safety. In visual nutrition estimation, Thames et al.~\cite{thames2021nutrition5k} released Nutrition5k for U.S. cafeteria meals, and Yan et al.~\cite{yan2025dietai24} developed DietAI24, combining multimodal LLMs, RAG, and FNDDS for image-based nutrient estimation. DietAI24 confirms that MLLM--RAG--database integration is already established for image workflows.

Recent 2026 studies directly investigate text-based dietary mapping and estimation. Lemay et al.~\cite{lemay2026foodmapper} evaluated hybrid semantic retrieval and LLM mapping (FoodMapper) on ASA24/FooDB (90.7\% accuracy) and NHANES/DFG2 (65.4\% accuracy). Zhou et al.~\cite{zhou2026nutrirag} introduced NutriRAG for retrieval-augmented classification of free-text food logs (micro-F1 82.24\%). Gjorgjevikj et al.~\cite{gjorgjevikj2026foodyllm} trained FoodyLLM on ~225,000 aligned nutrition QA pairs. Lawabni et al.~\cite{lawabni2026text} and Cao et al.~\cite{cao2026chinese} evaluated direct LLM text queries against the U.S. NCC database and the Chinese Food Composition Table, respectively, observing stronger agreement for energy and macronutrients than for micronutrients or composite dishes. In Taiwan, Chen et al.~\cite{chen2025diabot} validated Diabot-GPT-4o for image-based dietary assessment among 57 dietetic trainees against weighed food records. While these studies rule out broad claims that text mapping or Taiwanese AI dietary assessment is unstudied, none evaluate a Traditional Chinese text-first conversational agent with bounded clarification, deterministic provenance-preserving calculation, and manual-search comparison on Taiwanese mixed dishes.

\subsection{Nutrition Domain Knowledge and Safety Boundaries}
Reproducible nutrition calculation depends on preserving source energy definitions, raw/cooked states, and edible portions rather than silently overwriting database energy values with generic Atwater factors~\cite{fda_cfr1019}. Predictive equations such as Mifflin--St Jeor~\cite{mifflin1990ree} estimate resting energy expenditure (REE) rather than measuring individual metabolic rate, and the National Academies Energy DRI report highlights systematic measurement error in self-reported intake~\cite{nasem2023dri}. Local general reference guidance in Taiwan should follow verified Ministry of Health and Welfare HPA Dietary Reference Intakes (DRIs)~\cite{hpa_dri8}. Dynamic energy-balance modeling~\cite{hall2011bodyweight} demonstrates that bodyweight trajectories cannot be reduced to a linear single-day calorie rule, and inpatient or randomized trials~\cite{hall2019ultraprocessed,gardner2018dietfits,zeevi2015personalized} do not justify automated calorie prescriptions. Finally, systematic reviews examining diet-tracking apps and disordered-eating symptoms~\cite{roth2024selfmonitoring,anderberg2025apps} underscore the necessity of non-diagnostic, non-punitive guardrails aligned with clinical referral boundaries such as NICE NG69~\cite{nice2020ng69}. \section{Proposed Methodology}
\subsection{Design Principles and Trust Separation}
DietSense enforces three invariant principles: (1) \textbf{Deterministic Numeric Authority}---LLMs parse intent, food mentions, and portion spans and draft clarification prompts, but never fabricate nutrient values or override arithmetic; (2) \textbf{Null vs.\ Zero Distinction}---missing nutrient fields remain \texttt{null}/\texttt{unknown} with reason codes and are never imputed as $0$; and (3) \textbf{Field-Level Provenance}---every numeric output carries source ID, version/snapshot date, food ID, food state, unit, and formula version.

\subsection{Deterministic Nutrient Calculation}
For a verified food candidate $c$ with confirmed portion mass $m_c > 0$ (g) and source nutrient density $d_{c,k} \ge 0$ (kcal/100\,g for energy or g/100\,g for macronutrient $k$), the deterministic nutrient amount $N_{c,k}$ is:
\begin{equation}
N_{c,k} = m_c \cdot \frac{d_{c,k}}{100}.
\label{eq:scaling}
\end{equation}
For a multi-ingredient meal $M$ with verified components $i \in \{1, \dots, n\}$ and effective consumed mass $m_i = m_i^{\text{served}} (1 - \ell_i)$, where $\ell_i \in [0, 1)$ is an explicit leftover fraction, the meal total for nutrient $k$ is:
\begin{equation}
N_{M,k} =
\begin{cases}
\sum_{i=1}^{n} m_i \cdot \dfrac{d_{i,k}}{100}, & \text{if } d_{i,k} \neq \texttt{null}\ \forall i, \\[8pt]
\texttt{partial}\!\left(\sum_{i \in \mathcal{V}_k} N_{i,k}\right), & \text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
\label{eq:aggregation}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{V}_k = \{i : d_{i,k} \neq \texttt{null}\}$. Incomplete totals explicitly report coverage counts (\texttt{included}, \texttt{incomplete}, \texttt{excluded}) rather than treating missing components as zero.

\subsection{Evidence-Bounded Plausible Estimate Ranges}
When portion mass or candidate identity has explicit, source-backed lower and upper bounds $[m^{\text{low}}, m^{\text{high}}]$ and $[d_k^{\text{low}}, d_k^{\text{high}}]$ with $0 < m^{\text{low}} \le m^{\text{high}}$ and $0 \le d_k^{\text{low}} \le d_k^{\text{high}}$, DietSense computes an evidence-bounded plausible range:
\begin{equation}
R_k = \left[\frac{m^{\text{low}} d_k^{\text{low}}}{100},\; \frac{m^{\text{high}} d_k^{\text{high}}}{100}\right].
\label{eq:range}
\end{equation}
$R_k$ is an assumption-conditional interval and is \emph{never} labeled a statistical confidence interval absent external calibration. Retrieval ranking scores order candidates only and are never converted into percentage error bands.

\subsection{Optional Energy Reference Estimation}
Only when an eligible adult user ($\ge 18$ years, non-pregnant/lactating, no self-reported clinical diet therapy) explicitly requests an energy reference with complete anthropometric inputs, DietSense computes an optional REE estimate via Mifflin--St Jeor~\cite{mifflin1990ree}:
\begin{equation}
\text{REE}_{\text{est}} = 10 w + 6.25 h - 5 a + s, \quad s \in \{+5, -161\},
\label{eq:mifflin}
\end{equation}
where $w$ is weight (kg), $h$ is height (cm), $a$ is age (years), and $s$ is the published male/female constant. If a user-selected activity factor $\alpha$ is supplied, $\text{TDEE}_{\text{est}} = \text{REE}_{\text{est}} \cdot \alpha$ is reported as a secondary population estimate, never as a clinical prescription.

\subsection{Non-Blocking Multi-Intent and Clarification Algorithms}
Per the detailed system design specification, DietSense models clarification as an asynchronous cross-request state machine rather than a blocking server loop. Algorithm~\ref{alg:agent} formalizes request handling across all four intents (\texttt{log\_food}, \texttt{query\_nutrition}, \texttt{analyze}, \texttt{recommend}) with pre-/post-execution safety gates, while Algorithm~\ref{alg:resume} formalizes bounded clarification resumption with monotonic turn tracking ($t < T_{\max}$, default $T_{\max}=2$), explicit \texttt{skip}/\texttt{cancel}/\texttt{expired} termination, and conflict detection.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Non-Blocking Multi-Intent Request Handling}
\label{alg:agent}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require User input $u$, session state $S$ (policy $v_p$, max turns $T_{\max}$)
\Ensure Validated response envelope $E$, updated state $S'$
\State $\sigma_{\text{pre}} \gets \text{SafetyPreCheck}(u, v_p)$
\If{$\sigma_{\text{pre}}.\text{status} \in \{\texttt{refuse}, \texttt{restrict}, \texttt{refer}\}$}
    \State \Return $\text{RenderSafetyTemplate}(\sigma_{\text{pre}}), S$
\EndIf
\State $P \gets \text{ParseAndValidateSchema}(u)$
\If{$P.\text{status} \neq \texttt{success}$}
    \State \Return $\text{SafeParseFallback}(P), S$
\EndIf
\If{$P.\text{intent} = \texttt{log\_food}$}
    \State $C \gets \text{RetrieveVerifiedCandidates}(P.\text{entities})$
    \If{$\text{NeedsClarification}(P, C)$ \textbf{and} $S.t < T_{\max}$}
        \State $q \gets \text{SelectHighestImpactQuestion}(P, C)$
        \State $S' \gets \text{PersistMinimalState}(S, P, C, t{+}1)$
        \State \Return $\text{RenderClarification}(q, \texttt{allow\_skip}), S'$
    \EndIf
    \State $R \gets \text{DeterministicAnalytics}(P, C)$ \Comment{Eqs.~(\ref{eq:scaling})--(\ref{eq:range})}
\ElsIf{$P.\text{intent} = \texttt{query\_nutrition}$}
    \State $R \gets \text{AnswerFromVerifiedCandidates}(P.\text{query})$
\ElsIf{$P.\text{intent} = \texttt{analyze}$}
    \State $R \gets \text{AggregateDailyLog}(S.\text{log}, P.\text{date}, S.\text{tz})$
\ElsIf{$P.\text{intent} = \texttt{recommend}$}
    \State $R \gets \text{GeneralInfoIfAllowed}(S.\text{log}, \sigma_{\text{pre}}, v_p)$
\Else
    \State \Return $\text{UnsupportedIntentResponse}(), S$
\EndIf
\State $\sigma_{\text{post}} \gets \text{SafetyPostCheck}(R, \sigma_{\text{pre}}, v_p)$
\State $E \gets \text{RenderValidatedEnvelope}(R, \sigma_{\text{post}})$
\State \Return $E$ (with confirmation gate if draft log), $S'$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Bounded Clarification Resumption}
\label{alg:resume}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Clarification answer $a$, saved session state $S$
\Ensure Terminal or continued response envelope $E$, updated state $S'$
\If{$\text{IsExpired}(S)$ \textbf{or} $S.t \ge S.T_{\max}$}
    \State \Return $\text{TerminalPartialOrNoEstimate}(S), S.\text{log}$
\EndIf
\If{$a.\text{action} = \texttt{cancel}$}
    \State \Return $\text{CancellationResponse}(), S.\text{log}$
\ElsIf{$a.\text{action} = \texttt{skip}$}
    \State \Return $\text{PartialOrTextOnly}(S), S.\text{log}$
\EndIf
\State $S' \gets \text{ValidateAndApplyAnswer}(S, a)$;\quad $S'.t \gets S.t + 1$
\If{$\text{ConflictsWithPriorExplicit}(S', a)$}
    \If{$S'.t < S'.T_{\max}$}
        \State \Return $\text{AskResolveConflict}(S'), S'$
    \Else
        \State \Return $\text{TerminalPartial}(S'), S'.\text{log}$
    \EndIf
\EndIf
\State \Return $\text{ContinueRetrievalAndCalculation}(S')$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{System Architecture}
\subsection{Eight-Module Conceptual Architecture}
Consistent with the detailed system design specification, DietSense comprises eight logical modules (Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture}), separated from backing data stores:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{User / Application Layer (M1):} Captures Traditional Chinese meal descriptions, renders structured previews with provenance and assumptions, and enforces explicit user confirmation, edit, skip, and deletion controls.
    \item \textbf{Input Processing (M2):} Preserves raw text, normalizes whitespace/character width, extracts intent and food/portion/preparation spans, and validates outputs against versioned schemas.
    \item \textbf{Planner / Reasoning (M3):} Executes a finite-state workflow coordinating retrieval, bounded clarification, analytics, and terminal fallback states.
    \item \textbf{Data Retrieval (M4):} Queries licensed, versioned nutrition sources (USDA FDC, candidate TFDA database, curated local-recipe index) and returns ranked \texttt{NutritionCandidate} objects with field-level provenance.
    \item \textbf{Analytics Engine (M5):} Executes pure deterministic functions for unit validation, nutrient scaling, ingredient aggregation, daily summaries, and optional REE/TDEE estimation.
    \item \textbf{Domain Knowledge (M6):} Manages versioned unit conversions, source priority rules, population dietary guidance scope, and safety policies.
    \item \textbf{Decision / Recommendation (M7):} Combines calculation completeness and safety decisions (\texttt{allow\_general}, \texttt{restrict}, \texttt{refuse}, \texttt{refer}) to form permissible response plans.
    \item \textbf{Output / Interaction (M8):} Formats structured \texttt{ResponseEnvelope} payloads exposing units, sources, assumptions, unknown fields, and plausible ranges without post-hoc arithmetic.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Experimental Design}
All datasets, baselines, statistical analyses, and user studies below are prospective research plans; no data collection or experimentation has been executed.

\subsection{Research Questions}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{RQ1 (Estimation Accuracy):} On a fixed Traditional Chinese Taiwanese meal benchmark, how accurately does DietSense identify foods and estimate energy and available macronutrients relative to reference labels?
    \item \textbf{RQ2 (Clarification, Provenance, and Fallbacks):} When key fields are missing or matches are weak, does DietSense elicit bounded clarifications, preserve field-level provenance, and avoid unsupported numeric outputs?
    \item \textbf{RQ3 (Workflow Feasibility):} Compared against a version-locked manual-search logging baseline, how does DietSense perform in logging time, task completion, clarification burden, and subjective usability?
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Dataset Construction and Four-Tier Reference Labeling}
We plan a stratified dataset of 500 Traditional Chinese meal descriptions spanning six categories: (1) packaged/single foods, (2) Taiwanese bento and staple combinations, (3) noodle soups with broth/topping ambiguity, (4) night-market and street mixed dishes (e.g., oyster omelet, fried chicken cutlet, bubble tea), (5) home-cooked/local recipes, and (6) deliberately ambiguous, contradictory, or safety-boundary cases. Natural and synthetic descriptions will be tagged separately.

Recognizing that reference nutrition values are not error-free ground truth, each item will receive a four-tier reference level: \textbf{Tier~A} (verified packaged label or official database entry), \textbf{Tier~B} (standardized recipe with weighed ingredients and documented raw/cooked conversions), \textbf{Tier~C} (field-collected vendor meals with pre-/post-meal weighing and explicit oil/sauce uncertainty), and \textbf{Tier~D} (insufficient reference; evaluated solely for safe fallback/abstention and excluded from primary numeric error analysis). Two trained annotators will independently annotate entries using a frozen codebook, with a qualified dietitian adjudicating disagreements. Inter-annotator reliability will be reported via Cohen's $\kappa$ (categorical) and intraclass correlation coefficients (ICC) with Bland--Altman analysis (continuous). Group-aware splits by dish, recipe, vendor, and brand will prevent data leakage.

The primary baseline is a version-locked manual-search-and-entry workflow using identical meal tasks and reference sources. An analytical baseline (direct database lookup with manual formula application) and optional ablations (no clarification vs.\ bounded clarification vs.\ LLM-only error analysis) are planned. One primary outcome (fixed-benchmark calorie MAE or $\pm 20\%$ proportion) will be locked after pilot calibration; all remaining metrics will be designated secondary/exploratory. Paired task comparisons will use paired differences or mixed-effects models, with cluster-aware bootstrap confidence intervals for skewed error/time distributions and meal/vendor clustering. Candidate thresholds from the original proposal ($>85\%$ within $\pm 20\%$, 60\% shorter logging time, SUS $>70$) remain \emph{unvalidated exploratory targets} pending pilot calibration and protocol preregistration (freezing codebook, sampling list, source snapshots, prompts, formulas, and statistical code). Recruitment will not begin until institutional ethics/IRB approval, informed consent, privacy controls, and third-party LLM data-retention policies are finalized.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
Several methodological, technical, and governance limitations bound this proposal. First, our literature review was a targeted scoping search rather than a systematic review and did not directly query native IEEE Xplore or ACM Digital Library interfaces; novelty is therefore framed as a testable workflow hypothesis rather than a universal priority claim. Second, DietSense is currently unimplemented, and neither the 500-item dataset nor the 20-participant feasibility study has been executed. Third, text-only meal descriptions cannot directly observe hidden cooking oil, sauces, or unmeasured leftovers; some Taiwanese mixed dishes will legitimately degrade to \texttt{text\_only} or bounded ranges.

Fourth, DietSense is not a medical device and does not replace registered dietitians. Removing the original proposal's universal 1,200\,kcal/day cutoff avoids an unvalidated clinical rule, yet all replacement safety policies, referral templates, and consent-gated data-deletion workflows (covering primary logs, caches, and derived summaries) remain \texttt{pending\_professional\_review}. Fifth, a 20-participant, 7-day study evaluates operational feasibility only and cannot support population generalization, subgroup inference, or weight-loss efficacy claims.

Finally, two material inconsistencies across earlier project documents were resolved using the detailed system design specification: (1) the eight conceptual modules follow the logical architecture (M1--M8 in Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture}) rather than treating database and API gateway infrastructure as modules 7 and 8; and (2) clarification is formalized as a non-blocking cross-request state machine (Algorithms~\ref{alg:agent}--\ref{alg:resume} and Fig.~\ref{fig:statemachine}) rather than a blocking \texttt{while} loop.

\section{Conclusion}
This paper presented the research proposal, trust-bounded eight-module architecture, deterministic calculation methodology, paired non-blocking algorithms, and prospective evaluation design for DietSense, a Traditional Chinese conversational domain AI agent for dietary logging and nutrition analysis in Taiwan. By decoupling LLM-based language understanding from versioned nutrition retrieval, deterministic arithmetic, bounded clarification, and non-diagnostic safety guardrails, DietSense aims to make uncertainty and data provenance explicit in mixed-dish meal logging. Immediate next steps include auditing Taiwan food-composition source licensing and coverage, completing professional review of safety policies, preregistering the 500-item annotation protocol, and conducting pilot validation prior to prototype implementation and IRB-approved feasibility testing.
